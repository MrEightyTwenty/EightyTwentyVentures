Chapter 02

Risk-On Environments

Commercial participants in the futures market are the most informed actors in the equity complex. They are not trend-followers. They accumulate when conditions look worst and distribute when conditions look best. Following their positioning, rather than the financial media's interpretation of the same data, is the starting point for every position entry.

The Primary Condition: What Commercials Are Doing

The first and most important gate for initiating new position accumulation is confirmation that institutional commercial participants are actively buying equity futures into weakness. This is measured through a proprietary COT-based oscillator that reads the relationship between commercial and speculative positioning in the equity futures market.

When conditions are met, the oscillator marks the period green. When it indicates commercial distribution or speculative excess on the long side, the period is marked red. The signal's structure (a single bar or a sustained multi-bar range of green) also communicates something: a sustained signal spanning multiple periods indicates persistent commercial accumulation, which is a stronger basis for new thesis construction than a brief single-bar event.

Below the 20-Period Average: Stage Accumulation

The signal is most powerful, and most appropriate for initiating new position accumulation, when it fires while the S&P 500 is trading below its twenty-period moving average on the monthly or weekly chart. In these conditions, price is already dislocated below its recent mean. Retail sentiment is typically negative or fearful. The presence of commercial buying into that weakness is structurally significant: the participants who have the most information about true underlying value are accumulating while the broader market is distributing.

This combination (commercial accumulation below the 20-period average) is where the highest-conviction new position entries occur. The thesis is built here. Capital is deployed here.

Above the 20-Period Average: Pyramiding Context

When the oscillator produces a buy signal while the S&P 500 is above its 20-period average, the signal remains valid but its application changes. Above the average, a buy signal is a pyramiding opportunity for an existing position that has already been established and is developing correctly. It is not a trigger for a new thesis or a new stage accumulation.

Below the 20-Period Average

COT buy signal here = new thesis construction, stage accumulation, maximum conviction sizing. This is where the largest entries are made.

Above the 20-Period Average

COT buy signal here = pyramiding into existing positions that are already working. Not a basis for initiating new theses.

The Secondary Condition: Where Speculators Are Positioned

A reinforcing condition that elevates the primary signal is extreme speculative short positioning into weakness. When non-commercial trend-following participants are heavily leveraged short into a declining market at the same time commercials are accumulating, the conditions for a sharp and sustained reversal are at their most favorable.

Speculative short positioning represents deferred buying pressure. When the move reverses, those participants must cover their shorts. That covering pressure, layered on top of the commercial buying that initiated the reversal, amplifies and accelerates the move. The combination of maximum commercial accumulation and maximum speculative short exposure is not just a buy signal. It is a compression spring.

What the Signal Does Not Do

The COT oscillator identifies when the weight of institutional positioning has shifted sufficiently to justify deploying capital. It does not specify which sectors will benefit, how far price will extend, or how long the favorable environment will persist. Those questions are answered by the layers that follow. The oscillator's role is singular: confirming that conditions are favorable for the broader deployment of the thesis already constructed. Without the thesis, the signal has no direction to point in.

The Diagnostic Questions for This Stage

  • Is the COT oscillator currently producing or recently producing a green buy signal?
  • Is the S&P 500 below its 20-period moving average on the monthly or weekly chart?
  • Are speculative participants net short or near extreme short positioning?
  • Is the signal spanning multiple bars (sustained accumulation) or a single-bar event?
  • If price is above the 20-period average, is this a pyramid context for an existing position rather than a new entry?
