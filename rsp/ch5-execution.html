Chapter 05

Execution & Position Management

The market is either balanced or imbalanced. When it is imbalanced in the direction of the thesis, forward progress must develop. When it does not, the thesis is wrong regardless of how compelling the fundamental or macro analysis was. Invalidation is not a loss. It is information.

Entry Framework

Position entries are structured around two primary timeframe alignments. Monthly timeframe entries carry holding periods of 3 to 18 months and add on the weekly chart. Quarterly timeframe entries carry holding periods of 1 to 6 or more years and add on the monthly chart. LEAPs are sized as options positions with 3 to 12-month horizons using monthly timeframe triggers.

Order Types

Limit orders are used for entries on retracement legs, where price is returning to a prior structural level and the entry is anticipated. Market orders are reserved for moments of extreme volatility where a limit order would not be filled and the risk-to-reward remains acceptable at the market price. Chasing into a breakout with a market order at the wrong location is the most common way to convert a correct thesis into a poor trade.

Defining Invalidation Before Entry

Every position has a defined structural invalidation point established before the trade is entered. This is not a fixed percentage or arbitrary distance from entry. It is the specific price level at which the thesis is structurally disproven: typically a quarterly or yearly low where, if accepted, the structure that supported the original thesis no longer exists.

The Stop Is Not a Safety Net. It Is the Thesis Boundary.

A stop placed at a structural invalidation level is the point at which the analysis was wrong, not the point at which the loss becomes uncomfortable. Placing stops based on loss tolerance rather than structural logic means the position is being sized around a psychological threshold rather than an analytical one. These are different exercises with different outcomes.

Target Framework: Standard Deviation Extensions

Profit targets are constructed from standard deviation extensions of the retracement range entering the position. These are not arbitrary. They reflect the statistical distribution of price moves given the established range.

  • 1.0 Standard Deviation~68% probability pool. First target for partial exit or trailing stop adjustment. Represents a statistically normal extension.
  • 2.0 Standard Deviations~95% probability pool. Second target. A move to this level represents a statistically significant expansion beyond the prior range.
  • 3.0 Standard Deviations~99.7% probability pool. This is the full maturity target for the position. The 100-bagger mentality applied in practice: hold until the price action itself, not a predetermined profit target, says the move is complete.

LEAPs: Convexity and Implied Capability

Long-term equity anticipation securities (LEAPs) are used selectively to express high-conviction themes with defined maximum loss and leveraged upside. Their primary analytical value is what they reveal about market-implied price capability: the strike prices and expiration dates where options pricing implies the market sees meaningful probability of extension.

LEAPs are not used as a substitute for position analysis. They are used to exploit convexity in situations where the direction, timing, and magnitude of the move meet a sufficient convergence of evidence. A LEAP that expires worthless is a 1R loss with defined maximum exposure. A LEAP that catches a 3-standard-deviation move in a high-beta equity can return multiples. That asymmetry is the point.

Pyramiding Protocol

Additional size is added to an existing position only when new confirmation develops that validates the original thesis at a higher structural level. Two protocols apply.

Protocol 1: Top-Down Pyramid

Initial entry on the quarterly chart. First add when the quarterly timeframe produces a fresh signature. Second add when the weekly (sister timeframe) confirms. Stop for the original position remains at quarterly invalidation. Stop for the add shifts to weekly invalidation. Target remains the same if risk-to-reward is still favorable after both adds.

Protocol 2: Directional Pyramid

Initial entry on the monthly chart. First add on initial monthly confirmation. Second add when price re-confirms the trend on the monthly and creates a new structural invalidation point above the original entry. Stop 1 remains at original monthly invalidation. Stop 2 shifts to the new invalidation. Target remains the same if ratio is still favorable.

The absolute rule: no add increases total net risk. Every pyramid entry must either bring the overall position to breakeven or reduce net risk. Scaling into conviction means validating it structurally, not doubling down on a narrative.

Risk Parameters

  • Position SizingStandardized dynamic sizing using half or quarter Kelly Criterion. Kelly maximizes long-run geometric growth without accepting the full volatility of the full Kelly bet.
  • Risk per Trade2 to 8% of the book per position. Sized to the conviction quality and the alignment of all five system layers.
  • Risk-to-Reward Minimum1:2.5 minimum before any position is entered. Below this ratio, the opportunity does not justify the capital at risk.
  • Positions Per Month/Quarter2 to 6 total new positions. Scarcity forces selectivity. A position that barely qualifies should not be entered when the same capital could wait for one that clearly qualifies.
  • Maximum Drawdown3 to 7% of the book triggers a rebalancing review. At this point, the portfolio's correlation, sizing, and thesis integrity are audited before any new positions are initiated.
