Chapter 04

Sector, Industry & Equity Selection

Relative strength is the market's own verdict on which equities are being accumulated by informed participants. Fundamentals explain why a business will outperform. Technicals define when and where to own it. Both are necessary. Neither is sufficient alone.

Step 1: The Technical Scan for Relative Strength

Within the sectors identified through the theme branching process, the first screening criterion is relative strength. Price is the most efficient aggregator of all information about a stock. When a stock is outperforming its sector and the broader market during a period of general weakness, that outperformance is a signal that informed participants are accumulating, regardless of the current news narrative.

Technical signatures that indicate a stock is ready for examination:

  • Price sweeps a swing low, immediately reclaimsThe sweep generates liquidity. The immediate reclaim indicates buyers absorbed the selling and are prepared to push higher. This is the beginning of the PO3 (accumulation, manipulation, distribution) cycle becoming visible.
  • Reclaims 20-period average with structure confirmationThe 20-period average on the monthly or weekly chart represents the mean of recent value. Acceptance above it with volume at a month or quarter end indicates that the structural bias has shifted.
  • TTM Squeeze compressing then firingThe TTM Squeeze identifies periods where Bollinger Bands contract inside Keltner Channels. This compression indicates coiling energy. When it fires, the subsequent directional move tends to have significant range.
  • VolumeWeightMACD and OBV convergingVolume-weighted MACD diverging positively from price combined with On-Balance Volume trending upward indicates that volume is flowing into the stock during accumulation phases. Institutions buy quietly. Volume tells on them.

Step 2: Fundamental Assessment

Once the technical scan identifies relative strength candidates, each one undergoes fundamental analysis benchmarked against its own sector and industry, not against a universal template. A technology company's gross margin expectations are structurally different from a utility's. A financial's leverage profile is structurally different from an industrial's. Every fundamental metric is interpreted relative to what is normal and healthy within that specific sector.

Sector-Specific Evaluation Frameworks

Each sector has its own structural characteristics that make certain metrics more meaningful than the universal baseline. A healthcare company burning cash on R&D is not the same as an industrial company burning cash on overhead. The evaluation framework adjusts accordingly.

Technology

Prioritize: Revenue growth rate, Rule of 40 Score (Revenue Growth % + FCF Margin %), gross margin trajectory, and narrative ownership of the relevant secular theme. High R&D and CapEx are expected during expansion phases and create operating leverage later. Discount rate sensitivity is elevated for zero-real-yield environments.

Healthcare

Prioritize: Clinical and regulatory binary events, peak sales potential of lead assets (probability-adjusted), patent exposure versus new product pipeline, and reimbursement environment. Earnings are heavily dependent on regulatory outcomes. R&D expenditure is the primary value driver.

Financials

Prioritize: Interest rate level and yield curve shape, credit quality and loan loss trajectory, CET1 ratio and buyback capacity. Banks are leveraged macro bets. Return on equity is driven more by leverage than operational margin. Macro regime is the primary driver of performance.

Energy

Prioritize: Underlying commodity price (WTI, Brent, Henry Hub), breakeven cost and capital discipline, production growth vs. maintenance narrative. Earnings and cash flow are highly volatile and closely correlated to commodity prices. Valuations are best understood through Return on Employed Capital.

Industrials

Prioritize: Book-to-bill ratio and backlog size (total revenue visibility), global manufacturing PMI and CapEx cycles, labor and supply chain health. Revenue and cash flow are lumpy due to long-term project structures. Margin expansion typically comes through aftermarket and services revenue.

Step 3: Identifying the Individual Catalyst

Every high-conviction position has a specific reason to outperform beyond the macro theme and relative strength. The catalyst is the company-specific event or structural change that will force Wall Street to reprice the equity.

  • Return to profitabilityA company crossing from unprofitable to profitable forces a re-rating from speculative to investment-grade. New buyer pools enter the stock. Multiple expansion compounds earnings growth.
  • Forgotten by Wall StreetNo analyst coverage, low institutional ownership, and price action that ignores positive fundamentals creates the setup. The re-discovery of a stock that has been quietly improving creates a violent re-rating.
  • Innovative product or service launchA product that redefines a market or creates a new one entirely changes the earnings trajectory in ways that consensus estimates do not reflect.
  • Commodity cycle inflectionFor commodity-exposed companies, a sustained move in the underlying commodity price can transform a marginally profitable business into a highly profitable one with significant operating leverage.
  • Strategic acquisition or partnershipAccretive acquisitions that expand addressable market or add complementary capability change the long-term earnings power of the combined entity.
