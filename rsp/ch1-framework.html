Chapter 01

The System

Position trading is not a mechanism for generating frequent returns. It is the disciplined process of identifying rare, high-conviction opportunities and holding them through their full expansion. The philosophy is asymmetry. Not twenty to thirty slow growers, but three to seven concentrated bets that are probabilistic and widely asymmetric, held not to a comfort level but to their full maturity.

What This System Is Built to Do

This is a top-down, multi-layer position trading system for equities, ETFs, and related instruments. It begins at the broadest possible level, economic regimes, geopolitical forces, and capital flows, and progressively narrows that view into individual equities whose technical structure, fundamental quality, and market positioning make them the highest-probability vehicles for capturing what the broader environment is beginning to reward.

Entry into any position is gated behind a sequence of conditions that must align across multiple layers simultaneously: institutional participant behavior expressed through the futures market, a macro backdrop supporting the identified theme, a sector and industry structure positioned to benefit, a technically strong and fundamentally sound individual equity within that structure, and a price level that offers maximum asymmetry relative to a clearly defined invalidation point. No single layer is sufficient. The edge lives in their convergence.

The compounding effect of a small number of large winners drives the entirety of long-run performance. Most of the portfolio's return in any given year will come from one or two positions. The job is to find those positions, get into them at the right time, and hold them long enough for that to happen.

System Parameters

  • Holding PeriodsMonthly timeframe entries: 3 to 18 months. Quarterly timeframe entries: 1 to 6 or more years. LEAPs (options): 3 to 12 months. Positions are held for as long as the thesis remains intact, not to a predetermined time target.
  • Position Philosophy3 to 7 concentrated positions with genuine conviction. Each position is sized to matter. Marginal ideas with weak conviction do not earn allocation. The portfolio is not diversified for comfort. It is concentrated for impact.
  • Loss BehaviorLosses are cut quickly at structurally defined levels before permanent impairment develops. A 1R loss is the cost of a wrong thesis. A deeply impaired position is a choice. The two are not equivalent.
  • Win BehaviorWinners are held through discomfort and into distribution. The impulse to exit a winning position before it has reached structural maturity is one of the most consistent destroyers of long-run returns in position trading. Resist it structurally, not just psychologically.

The Five Business Qualities That Create Outsized Returns

Investing in a stock is investing in a business. The businesses that produce outsized returns in favorable environments share five structural characteristics. These are not the only things that matter in equity analysis, but the absence of any one of them is a meaningful warning sign.

1. Profitable

The company generates consistent earnings beyond all costs. Profitability is not just the current year's net income; it is the demonstrated ability to earn more than it spends across a cycle. True earnings growth can be decomposed into: Volume Growth (change in sales volume) times Price Lever (change in price per unit) times Operating Leverage (change in EBIT per unit of sales change) times Financial Leverage (change in EPS per unit of EBIT change).

2. Efficient

The company optimizes resources to maximize operational output and minimize waste. Efficiency shows up in gross margin trajectory, return on invested capital, and the ratio of operating expenses to gross profit. A business that generates $1 of profit from $1.20 of resources is structurally superior to one that requires $2.00.

3. Financially Healthy

The company has liquidity, manageable leverage, and structural stability. An Altman Z-Score above 2.99 suggests financial health. Net Debt to EBITDA below 2.0x indicates reasonable leverage. Operating Cash Flow exceeding Current Liabilities indicates near-term solvency. Companies that cannot survive a bad year cannot compound across multiple good ones.

4. Confidently Managed

Owner-operators and founder-led companies with meaningful insider ownership tend to allocate capital with longer time horizons and greater discipline than hired management. Insider ownership aligns incentives in a way that quarterly earnings pressure does not. Look for consistent reinvestment decisions, not just strong current returns.

5. Future-Growth Oriented

The company reinvests wisely in R&D, capital expenditures, and market expansion. The size of the addressable market matters. A business with a 30% market share of a $500M market has a different return profile than one with 3% of a $50B market. Growth reinvestment that shrinks addressable market is value destruction dressed as ambition.

Companies that undergo periods of distress before new management arrives and flips the fundamental trajectory often produce explosive returns. They go from being under-analyzed and ignored by Wall Street to being re-rated at new valuations by Wall Street. The entry occurs during the ignored phase.

The Condensed Operating Philosophy

  • Wait for commercial participants to accumulate below the mean before deploying capital
  • Map the regime. Identify the dominant theme. Branch it to the sector and then the industry
  • Find the strongest equity in the right sector at the right point in the cycle
  • Confirm the fundamental catalyst. Define invalidation before entry
  • Enter with precision. Hold with discipline. Cut without sentiment when the structure says so
  • Let winners compound to their full maturity, not to a comfort level
  • Review everything honestly. Repeat
