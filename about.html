The origin.

At 19, I thought I knew exactly who I was going to become.

I was in college studying to be a physical therapist and was actively walking onto a Division-I soccer program, chasing a lifelong dream of competing at the highest level. Soccer had always been my outlet. It became a way to express myself, to test my limits, and to build something meaningful through discipline.

When I didn't make the team, it left a void I wasn't prepared for. For the first time, I was forced to confront a simple but uncomfortable truth: if I couldn't pursue the path I had built my identity around, I needed to find something else that would challenge me, capture my curiosity, and shape my future.

What began as curiosity quickly turned into obsession.

Around that same time, I discovered financial markets, starting (like many do) in the deep end with 0DTE options. The promise of fast returns and constant action was intoxicating, but the reality was more than humbling. I lost, repeatedly. The market didn't reward excitement, effort, or confidence. It rewarded understanding, and I didn't have it yet.

I wanted to understand why markets moved, why some people consistently succeeded while others failed, and what actually separated durable and repeatable success from short-term luck. That pursuit led me deeper, away from short-term speculation and into futures, equities, and longer-term instruments like LEAP options, where positioning, patience, and probability mattered more than impulse.

Over time, I realized markets were more than just charts or trades. They were reflections of expectations, collective behaviors, capital flows, and decision-making around future uncertainty. Studying them became my new competitive arena. The same discipline I had once poured into a sport was redirected into research, journaling, and building frameworks to navigate uncertainty.

EightyTwenty was born from that journey.

Not from instant success, but from failure, adaptation, and the realization that the most valuable skill isn't predicting every outcome. It's learning how to think independently, manage risk, and position yourself for when opportunity appears.

What started as a search for a new identity became a career, and ultimately, a mission to help others shorten the learning curve that once felt impossibly steep.

The Mission

What this is, and what it isn't.

What it is

A framework ecosystem.

A structured system for developing independent, context-aware market participants. Built entirely by traders, for traders, grounded in real-world market experience rather than theory alone. Tools, curriculum, and community, designed to build judgment rather than dependency.

What it isn't

A signal service.

No trade alerts. No daily picks. No day-trading room. The goal is to develop participants who can read the same inputs and arrive at their own high-conviction conclusions. The thinking is the product. The decisions are always yours.

What it is

A place for students, not performers.

This is not a place for ego, arrogance, or performative expertise. Everyone involved remains a student of the markets regardless of experience level. The philosophy is "each one, teach one": members help elevate one another through shared learning, honest trade reviews, and real accountability.

What it isn't

A lifestyle brand.

No P&L screenshots. No performance claims. No urgency. The brand is built around intellectual depth and framework quality, not social proof or status signaling.
