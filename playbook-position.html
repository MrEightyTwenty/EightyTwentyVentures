Longer-Term · Position

The Relative-Strength Playbook

A top-down position trading system. Six chapters covering the full process from identifying when institutional participants are accumulating, through mapping the macro regime, selecting the highest-probability equities, and managing positions to their full maturity.

Six Chapters

The complete process.

This is not a system for generating frequent returns. It is for identifying rare, high-conviction opportunities and holding them through their full expansion. Read sequentially first. Return to chapters as reference points throughout a trade's life.

Chapter 01

The Philosophy of Asymmetry

Why exceptional returns require concentrated, unconventional positioning. The power-law nature of equity returns, what transitional companies look like before they become obvious, and the philosophical shift from trader to capital allocator.

 Chapter 02

Reading Accumulation

How institutional participants build exposure before narratives shift, why markets bottom on improving expectations rather than good news, relative strength as a capital flow signal, and volatility compression as the precursor to expansion.

 Chapter 03

Mapping the Macro Regime

Economic cycles, geopolitical forces, and capital flow dynamics. How global conditions translate into themes, and how themes branch into sectors, industries, and individual opportunities.

 Chapter 04

Financial Intelligence

Reading businesses beneath the chart. The five qualities that distinguish exceptional companies, why cash flow trumps reported earnings, and how sector context changes what every metric means.

 Chapter 05

Corporate Life Cycles

Every company evolves through recognizable stages. The market values them differently at each one. Understanding where a company sits in its life cycle determines which metrics matter and what return profile is realistically available.

 Chapter 06

Building the Portfolio

Concentration as a feature not a bug, sizing by conviction quality, defining invalidation before entry, holding to structural maturity instead of a comfort level, and the review cadence that replaces continuous monitoring.
