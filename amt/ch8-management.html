Chapter 08

Trade Management

Going broke taking profits is a real phenomenon. Premature exits steal from the right tail of the distribution that the frequent small losses depend on to sustain positive expectancy. Follow the trade plan. Do not cheat your future self out of the outcome your past self did the work to set up.

The EMA Ribbon: Permission to Keep Holding

When a trade is working and the market shows structural potential for a larger move, the five-minute 9-20 EMA ribbon serves as the holding mechanism. Rather than taking profits at an arbitrary target or flinching at the first pullback, the position is held as long as the market continues to respect the ribbon. The ribbon is not a stop. It is a structural permission slip to continue holding.

Ribbon Holding Logic

Once directional momentum is confirmed and moving, hold toward the next structural area where the opposing side is expected to appear. If that area has not been reached and the five-minute ribbon remains intact (price has not broken and closed through the 9-20 EMA band against the trade direction), the position stays on.

Two conditions end the hold, whichever arrives first: price reaches the original target zone, or price breaks and closes through the ribbon, signaling that structural momentum has ended.

The practical effect: this approach allows the market to tell you when the trade is over rather than imposing an arbitrary exit. On genuine trend days, this converts what would have been a 2R winner into a 5-7R one. The system's overall expectancy depends on allowing that to happen without interference.

Pyramiding Through Structural Confirmation

Pyramiding is a strategic extension of correct positioning, not emotional averaging. Additional size is added only when price retraces to a prior five-minute area where confirmed one-sided order flow was visible on the footprint, and where a fresh signature consistent with the original thesis appears. The retrace into that area indicates the dominant side is reloading rather than exiting.

The non-negotiable rule: no pyramid entry is permitted if it increases total trade risk. Every add must either bring the existing position to breakeven or improve the overall risk profile. Adding to a winning trade while simultaneously increasing total exposure is not pyramiding. It is a different activity with very different consequences.

Pyramid Requirements

  • Price retraces to a five-minute area with confirmed prior tape imbalance, visible in footprint delta
  • A fresh footprint and DOM signature consistent with the original thesis is developing at the retrace location
  • The add location allows the existing position to reach breakeven or better on a subsequent stop trigger
  • Total net risk after the add is equal to or less than total risk before it

Sizing: Pressing What You Have Earned

Position size is a direct function of how many confluence layers are aligned. Full alignment justifies maximum conviction sizing. Partial alignment justifies reduced size. Two layers or fewer is generally not a standard-size trade and may not be a trade at all.

Session PeriodPriorityFocus
AM SessionPrimaryBoth continuation and reversal trades; full sizing when layers align
PM SessionSecondaryReversion and reversal setups; reduced sizing; compressed development windows

Expectancy = (Average Win x Win Rate) − (Average Loss x Loss Rate)

A 40% win rate with a 4:1 average payoff ratio produces higher expectancy than a 70% win rate at 1:1. They feel completely different to trade, which is precisely why most people gravitate toward the wrong one. The low-win-rate system requires accepting more frequent small losses and holding winners through discomfort. That behavioral requirement is why it remains available as an edge.

The retail advantage of complete time independence means there are no quotas, mandates, or external benchmarks. This enables extreme selectivity: waiting for the unambiguous setup and engaging only when the odds are genuinely tilted. Use it deliberately and without apology. No trade is preferable to a low-quality trade. This is not a cliche. It is math.
