Chapter 02

Framing a Market

The objective is not to predict direction. It is to determine where the market has recently accepted or rejected value, and where it is likely to make its next meaningful decision. Equally important: where is the market probably not going? Spend equal time on both. The second question is routinely underweighted.

Before the Session Opens

By the time RTH begins, the market has already communicated a substantial amount. Prior sessions have left volume distributions, accepted levels, rejected zones, and directional footprints across higher timeframes. The pre-market process is the work of reading that communication systematically, then constructing conditional responses rather than predictions. The difference matters: predictions require being right. Conditional responses require having read the options correctly.

The process runs in three sequential stages. Each narrows the context and hands off to the next. By the end, the session narrative is a set of if-then scenarios, not a forecast. The market will tell you which scenario it has chosen to pursue. Your job is to have responses ready, not to have been right in advance.

Stage 1: Structural Context

The starting point is the daily chart paired with a rolling five-day volume profile. The objective is simple: where has the market spent meaningful time and volume recently, and where has it refused to transact? Volume is the primary lens here, and it reveals two non-negotiable truths.

What Volume Reveals That Price Cannot

1. Where the market is and is not interested in conducting business.

2. The inventory status and positioning of dominant participants.

In a trending structure, volume builds directionally because insufficient opposing interest exists to slow the move. In a balanced structure, volume accumulates across a range in a D-shaped distribution. Those two conditions call for completely different session approaches. Treating a balanced market like a trending one is one of the most reliable ways to donate capital to someone who correctly identified the difference.

Stage 2: Overnight Session Analysis

Once structural context is established, focus shifts to how the Globex session is interacting with that broader framework. Overnight highs, lows, midpoints, and volume concentrations are cross-referenced against the daily and five-day anchors. Globex reflects global capital flows, risk adjustments, and early institutional positioning. It is not noise. It is context with a specific language you learn to read over time.

Overnight Behaviors and Their Implications

Sustained acceptance above prior value: Buyers comfortable at elevated prices; continuation toward composite highs is the lean.

Repeated failure at value area edges: Neither side can sustain a break; balance continues or reversal develops.

Large overnight imbalance with thin structure: Vulnerable inventory. These levels frequently unwind after RTH volume enters.

Passive probing without commitment: Indecision. Let RTH establish the narrative before positioning.

Stage 3: Real-Time Adaptation

The pre-market work is a hypothesis. Once the session begins, that hypothesis is tested against reality. The market either validates the prepared scenario or it doesn't. When it doesn't, the pre-market narrative does not override what is actually happening. Real-time evidence wins. Always. This is not a close call.

After 2 PM, the volume profile built during the morning session becomes the primary intraday reference structure. The morning's value area, point of control, and extremes are now fresh inflection points for afternoon decision-making.

When the market is literally showing you where the interest is going, do yourself a favor and don't take it for a liar. It has no interest in your opinions. It has its own, and those are the only ones with authority in this room.

The Questions Worth Asking Every Session

  • Where has the market spent the most time and volume in recent sessions?
  • Where has it repeatedly refused to go?
  • Is current price operating within, above, or below the dominant value area?
  • Is recent structure compressing or expanding directionally?
  • What does the overnight session suggest about inventory heading into RTH?
  • Where is the market probably not going, and what structural evidence supports that conclusion?
