Chapter 06

Don't Take the Market for a Liar

Always be aware of which decisions have already been made and what the likely next step is. What MGI reference is the market headed toward, and where did it come from? Intraday trading is a decision-sequencing exercise. Not a level-hunting one.

The Reference Levels That Matter

The intraday framework operates through a specific set of price levels representing decisions the market has already made, or has yet to make. Each acceptance or rejection of these levels closes one set of scenarios and opens another. Tracking this sequence is how the session's probability distribution narrows progressively throughout the day. The levels below are grouped by what they actually represent, rather than presented as an undifferentiated list.

Where Yesterday Ended

  • NY Prior Day High / Low (PDH / PDL)The extremes of the most recently completed Regular Trading Hours session. The most consistently reactive reference across all participant groups intraday.
  • Globex Prior Day High / LowThe extremes of the most recently completed overnight session specifically, distinct from the RTH range. These can sit inside, outside, or overlap with the NY prior day range, and the relationship between the two says something about how the overnight session traded relative to the day before it.
  • PWH / PWLPrior week high and low. Carry elevated structural weight when aligned with intraday zone activity.

What Globex Built Overnight

  • ONH / ONLOvernight high and low for the session currently in progress. The outer limits of tonight's Globex activity. RTH interaction with these levels is often the session's first meaningful decision.
  • Globex POCThe point of control for the overnight session. Frequently acts as a gravitational reference or pivot point after RTH opens.
  • Globex VAH / VALThe value area high and low of the overnight session: the boundaries of where the overnight session did the bulk of its business. Price entering RTH inside, above, or below this range is itself an early piece of context.

What Today Has Built So Far

  • RTH OpenThe price at which regular session participants begin operating. Sustained acceptance above or below it with volume commits a directional lean for the session.
  • 30-Min Opening Range (ORH / ORL) and ExtensionsThe first 30 minutes of RTH define the opening range. Acceptance above ORH or below ORL signals directional intent has been declared. The extensions, equal-distance projections beyond the range in the direction of the break, are the levels the market frequently references next once that intent is confirmed.
  • Initial Balance (IBH / IBL) and ExtensionsThe first hour of RTH defines the initial balance. A market that holds inside its initial balance is communicating something different than one that extends through it early. The IB extensions, like the opening range extensions, are the next logical reference points once the IB has been broken with volume.

The Midpoints

Midpoints matter because they mark the boundary between "closer to the high" and "closer to the low" for whatever range they belong to. A market trading above the midpoint of a given range is, by definition, spending more time in the upper half of that range's negotiation, and vice versa. These are quiet levels. They rarely produce dramatic reactions on their own, but they are useful for gauging which half of a given range's debate the market currently favors.

  • Midpoint of the Daily RangeThe midpoint of the most recently completed daily range, or the developing one.
  • Midpoint of the Weekly RangeThe midpoint of the most recently completed or developing weekly range.
  • Midpoint of the Monthly RangeThe midpoint of the most recently completed or developing monthly range.
  • Midpoint of the Opening RangeThe midpoint of the 30-minute opening range. A useful early gauge of whether the first half hour leaned toward its highs or its lows.
  • Midpoint of the Initial BalanceThe midpoint of the first hour's range. Frequently revisited later in the session as price decides which half of the morning's negotiation it ultimately favors.

Tracking the Decision Chain

Intraday sessions are not prediction exercises. They are decision-tracking exercises. Each acceptance or rejection at a key level produces information that constrains what is likely to happen next.

A Decision Chain in Practice

Event 1: Price pushes into NY PDH, gets rejected cleanly. Known: participants are not accepting trade above prior day range at this time.

Event 2: Falls back through ONH and RTH open without holding either. Known: both overhead references ceded; structural pressure shifted lower.

Event 3: Price reaches ORL, finds a bid, holds. Known: the downside probe found participants; lower prices are not being accepted either.

Event 4: Price reclaims ORL with volume and stays above it. Known: ORL has shifted from resistance to support.

Event 5: Price works through RTH open, ONH, then NY PDH. Known: NY PDH accepted above; directional intent confirmed through sequential evidence.

At each step, the prior decision is settled. The only open question is what happens next given what has been demonstrated. By midday you are not working with the same uncertainty you had at 9:31 AM.

Failure to Sustain Is a Decision

A market that probes above a reference level and immediately returns below it is communicating the same information as a market that rejects sharply at that level. Both indicate that participants were unwilling to accept trade at those prices. The absence of a dramatic reversal candle does not make the rejection less real. Treat failure to sustain as the signal it is.

Levels Change Function When Accepted

Once price transacts above a previously significant level with volume over a sustained duration, that level changes its structural role. Prior resistance accepted above becomes the floor of the new range. Prior support accepted below becomes the ceiling. Tracking which levels have changed function is essential to understanding where the market currently is, not just where it has been.

The market cannot fake genuine acceptance. Volume transacting at a level over time is acceptance by definition. If that turns out to be wrong, price will return below the level and the evidence will update. Read what is actually happening. Adjust accordingly. There is no shame in updating a view. There is significant financial shame in refusing to.
